## 3. The order bump

Follows the Emerald pattern exactly: **single checkbox, single option, same visit,
marginal-cost logic, hard deadline.** Kyle's price point, William's modality, Emerald's
economics.

### The pricing case

| | Emerald (proven ~50% take) | Kyle's proposal | William's proposal | Recommended |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offer | Under-Chin Sculpt | Choice of IV, red light **or** cryo | Red light, 1 session | **Red Light Recovery Pack, 3 sessions** |
| Anchor | $97 | not set | $40 | $120 |
| Price | $47 | $47 | $25 | **$47** |
| Discount | 52% | n/a | 37.5% | 61% |
| Saving | $50 | n/a | $15 | $73 |

**On price, Kyle is right and William's number is too low.** A $15 saving does not move
people. $50+ does. Going to a 3-session pack restores Emerald's exact price point and
discount depth on William's preferred modality, and adds something neither version has:
**two reasons to come back**. Sessions 2 and 3 are return visits, and a return visit is
where the program conversation actually happens.

### On offering a choice of three

Kyle's version lets the patient pick between an IV, a red light treatment, or cryo. We are
recommending against the choice, for one reason: **the thing that makes Emerald's bump
convert at ~50% is that it is a single checkbox.** The decision is yes or no. Adding a
three-way choice turns one easy decision into two harder ones - "do I want an add-on" and
"which add-on is best for me" - at the exact moment we are asking for a card. Bump take
rates fall when the bump requires comparison.

The three-modality idea is not wrong, it is just in the wrong place. It belongs **after**
the first visit, once the clinician knows the patient. Red light is the right single bump
now because it is the cheapest to deliver, needs no clinical screening, and stacks onto a
session the patient is already lying down for. IV and cryo both carry more delivery cost
and more clinical judgement, which makes them better as a post-visit upsell than as a
pre-visit checkbox.

If we want to test the choice later, the clean way is to run it as a v2 against the
single-option control once we have a take-rate baseline.

## 4. The guarantee

Emerald's First-Inch Guarantee works because a 3D scanner settles it in one visit.
**HBOT has no equivalent same-day measurement, and we should not invent one.** HRV does
not reliably move in a single session, and guaranteeing a physiological change on a
physician-led clinic's page is both dishonest and a regulatory problem.

So the risk reversal moves from outcome to experience:

> ### The Worth-It Guarantee
> Finish your session. If you walk out and do not think it was worth $147, say so at the
> front desk before you leave and we refund it on the spot. No form, no argument, no
> "let us try one more."

Same price point and same refund mechanic the clinic already honours on Emerald, so this
is not a new operational commitment. It is honest, it is strong, and it makes no medical
claim.

## 5. Compliance guardrails - read before writing any ad or page

HBOT marketing is an active FDA enforcement area. Three things must change from the
current page:

1. **Remove the Lyme disease testimonial.** The FDA has issued specific consumer warnings
   about HBOT marketed for Lyme. This is the single highest-risk item currently live.
2. **Do not attach "FDA-cleared" to energy, focus, or recovery.** Hyperbaric chambers are
   cleared for a defined list of conditions; fatigue and brain fog are not on it. If the
   phrase is used at all it describes the *device*, never the benefit being sold.
3. **Verify or drop "Over 10,000+ clinical studies."** Unsourced and easy to challenge.

Ongoing rules for every asset in this funnel:

- Never: cure, treat, heal, reverse, fix. Always: support, recovery, how you feel.
- Testimonials describe **experience** ("I had more energy by week two"), never a disease
  outcome.
- Meta: no diagnostic framing. "Tired of running on empty?" implies a personal health
  attribute. Use aspirational framing instead - "Recover like you did ten years ago."
- Every page carries a visible line: *Individual results vary. This is a wellness service
  and is not a substitute for medical care.*
